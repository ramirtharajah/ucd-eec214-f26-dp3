Calculate the appropriate inductance needed for the buck converter to operate at the edge of continuous conduction mode (CCM) for a load current of 4 mA. Calculate the appropriate load capacitance to meet the steady-state output voltage ripple specification. Choose the power FET dimensions to meet the efficiency specification including the power consumption of the gate drive inverters shown in Figure 1. The recommended sizing for the gate drivers is to follow a fanout of four rule of thumb, e.g., if your PMOS and NMOS power FETs are 16×16\times minimum size, then the inverter driving the power FET gates should be 4×4\times minimum size and the first inverter should be 1×1\times minimum size. Assume a minimum-sized inverter has WP/WN=1350nm/450nmW_{P}/W_{N} = 1350\ \textrm{nm}/450\ \textrm{nm} and uses minimum channel lengths.

Inductor and Capacitor ESR

The equivalent series resistance (ESR) of the inductor and capacitor are functions of the inductance and capacitance. Use the following equations to calculate the ESR values for both component values you calculated above.

For the inductor, the ESR is directly proportional to the inductance: ESRL=kLL\begin{equation} \label{eq:ESRL} \textrm{ESR}_{L} = k_{L}L \end{equation}

where kLk_{L} = 14 mΩ\Omega/μ\muH and LL is in μ\muH.

For the capacitor, losses are a function of the dissipation factor (DF) and the capacitance. The dissipation factor depends on the capacitor dielectric material and is often a function of frequency. If the DF is relatively small, the ESR can be modeled as directly proportional to the impedance of the capacitor at the switching frequency:

ESRC=DF⋅12πfSWC\begin{equation} \label{eq:ESRC} \textrm{ESR}_{C} = \textrm{DF}\cdot \frac{1}{2\pi f_{SW} C} \end{equation}

Assuming a multi-layer ceramic capacitor (MLCC) with a Y5V ceramic dielectric, the dissipation factor DF = 9%.

Transient Simulations

Download the ZIP archive of the design project: DP3.zip. Unzip the archive in the directory where you would like to edit and simulate the project. Open the project using OrCAD. You should see a one-sheet schematic similar to the page below with the same default values for the design parameters filled in. Complete the project by editing all the parameters such as WnUnitW_{nUnit}, MM, etc., setting them equal to your desired values. Include a legible image or pdf of your schematic as part of your report (see below).

Finish implementing your design using OrCAD schematic capture and verify it using PSpice simulation using the same library and device models used in the OrCAD PSpice tutorials. The testbench should include the voltage sources that provide the PWM control signals for your power FETs. The 0-100% rise and fall times for these voltage sources should be 1 ns. Note that you should use two voltage sources because (1) DCM operation requires both power FETs to be off during part of the switching period and (2) you may want to ensure a brief (1-2 ns) nonoverlap time to minimize short circuit current and improve efficiency. Simulate your circuit using transient analysis at each load current from 1 to 10 mA, at increments of 1 mA. The transient analysis should run for 1 ms to ensure the circuit reaches steady state operation. Plot the startup transient waveforms for the output voltage VOUTV_{OUT} and the inductor current ILI_{L} for the load conditions IL=4I_{L} = 4, 10, and 1 mA. Also, plot at least two cycles of the steady-state transient waveforms for VOUTV_{OUT} and ILI_{L} at the same three load conditions. Include these plots in your project report.

Because there is no feedback loop to regulate the output voltage, you will need to calculate the appropriate duty cycle at each of the 10 load currents. Below 4 mA, the converter should be operating in discontinuous conduction mode (DCM) and you will need to calculate a different duty cycle at each load current. At 4 mA and above, the converter is operating in CCM. Verify through transient simulation that the output voltage steady-state value and ripple meets the specifications.

Efficiency

Plot the efficiency as a function of load current. At which load is the efficiency maximized? Explain why the efficiency is a maximum at that load for your design.
